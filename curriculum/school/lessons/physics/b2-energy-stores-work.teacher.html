1. Purpose and objectives

Block intent (programme manifest): a precise language for energy (stores and pathways, never “forms”), conservation in a closed system, and work done as energy transferred by a force.

This block builds the qualitative model that every later Energy calculation depends on (Block 3 links Ek and Ep by conservation; Block 5 uses dissipation for efficiency). It is also the first time pupils use a recall equation, W = F s, with the programme’s calculation routine. Ek = ½mv² and Ep = mgh are not taught here (Block 3): no question in this block needs them, and all energies in store-to-store questions are given as numbers.

Success criteria (as the pupil sees them)

By the end pupils can: define a system and name the eight stores; name the four pathways; describe all store changes for the five AQA situations (projected upwards, hitting an obstacle, accelerated by a constant force, vehicle slowing down, kettle); state conservation and explain dissipation; read and interpret a Sankey diagram drawn to scale; calculate W = F s and rearrange for F or s with the distance along the line of action of the force; explain that work done against friction causes heating.

3. Language rules

From the first minute: model “Energy is transferred from the … store of … to the … store of … by …”. Name every store in full (“the kinetic energy store of the car”). Correct loose language every time, calmly and at once; ask the pupil to say the sentence again.
Pupils say (never accept as a final answer)Why it is wrongSay instead

“electrical energy”Electrically is a pathway, not a store.“energy is transferred electrically (by the current)”
“light energy”, “sound energy”Light and sound are ways energy is carried, not stores.“transferred by radiation (light)”; “a very small amount is transferred by sound”

The “lost to where?” move. Pupil: “The energy is lost.” Teacher: “Lost to where?” Pupil: “To the surroundings.” Teacher: “Which store of the surroundings?” Pupil: “The thermal energy store.” Teacher: “Say the whole sentence.” Pupil: “The energy is dissipated to the thermal energy store of the surroundings.” Keep going until the full sentence is said; do not finish it for them.

Acceptable words: “wasted” and “dissipated” for energy transferred to less useful stores. The only places banned phrases appear in the pupil file are the struck-through “never say” list, the quoted wrong statements in the misconception clinic (Pupil A in C1 and C3, Pupil B in C2 and C4) and the sentence pupils rewrite in exit ticket E3, where they are there to be corrected.

4. Calculation routine (programme-wide, 7 steps)

  1. GIVEN: list every quantity with its symbol and unit. Convert to SI units now (kg, m, s, J, W).
  2. FIND: write the symbol of the quantity you need.
  3. RELATIONSHIP: write the equation in symbols (from memory, or from the equation sheet if it is a given equation).
  4. REARRANGE: make the unknown the subject before putting numbers in.
  5. SUBSTITUTE: put the numbers in. This line usually earns the first mark.
  6. SOLVE: calculate. Give the unit, and the significant figures asked for.
  7. CHECK: is the size sensible? Is the unit right for the quantity?

W = F s is a recall equation (not on the AQA equation sheet). It is printed in the pupil file only in the teaching text, the worked examples and as labelled hints in guided practice. It is not printed in retrieval, independent practice, the exit ticket, the Grade 9 challenge or the homework. Do not write it on the board during those parts. Symbol warning: W is work done here, but also weight (W = mg) and the unit watt (W); s is distance here but also the unit second.

8. Complete answers and mark schemes: Session 1

Marking principles: for Calculate questions, 1 mark for correct substitution (or rearrangement), 1 for the answer, and where shown 1 for a conversion; allow error carried forward (ECF) from an earlier step. A correct final answer with a correct unit and no working gets full marks unless the question asks for working. A wrong or missing unit on the final answer loses the answer mark only when the unit is not given in the question. Answers using “heat energy”, “movement energy” or “electrical energy” as store names, or “lost/used up” as the destination, do not earn the mark for that point.

1.3 Worked examples (faded parts)

Example 2 (coconut) Step 2: from the gravitational potential energy store of the coconut–Earth system to the kinetic energy store of the coconut. Step 3: to the thermal energy stores of the coconut, the sand and the surroundings (dissipated); a very small amount by sound. Step 4: the total energy of the system (coconut, Earth, sand, air) is unchanged; energy has been transferred, not destroyed.

Example 4 (lift) REARRANGE s = W ÷ F; SUBSTITUTE s = 86 400 J ÷ 7200 N; SOLVE s = 12 m; CHECK J ÷ N = m, and 12 m is about four storeys, which is sensible.

2.2 Misconception clinic (each 2 marks: 1 for choosing the correct pupil, 1 for the reason)

C1 (MIS-PHY-ENE-001) Pupil B. Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It was transferred electrically from the chemical energy store of the battery to the phone’s circuits and then by radiation (the screen’s light) and by heating; it ends up dissipated in the thermal energy store of the surroundings. The total is unchanged.

C2 (MIS-PHY-ENE-002) Pupil A. “Electrical” describes a pathway (energy is transferred electrically), not a store, and “heat” is not a store: the store that increases is the thermal energy store of the water. Energy is not changed from one kind into another; it is transferred between stores.

C3 (MIS-PHY-ENE-003) Pupil B. Friction cannot destroy energy. Friction does work on the skateboard, transferring energy from its kinetic energy store to the thermal energy stores of the wheels, the ground and the air: they warm slightly. The energy is dissipated.

C4 (MIS-PHY-FOR-001) Pupil A. In W = F s, s is the distance moved along the line of action of the force. The upward force is vertical, but the load moves horizontally and stays at the same height, so the distance along the force is 0 m and W = 200 N × 0 m = 0 J. (If pupils ask: the porter’s body does transfer energy while walking, but not by this upward force on the load.)

2.4 Independent and Grade 9 challenge

I1 tugboat [3] s = 3.0 km = 3.0 × 10³ m (1); W = 2.0 × 10⁵ N × 3.0 × 10³ m = 6.0 × 10⁸ J (1); = 600 MJ (1). Watch: 6.0 × 10⁵ J (km not converted, MIS-PHY-SKL-002); 6.0 × 10¹⁴ MJ or 6.0 × 10² J (prefix conversion the wrong way, MIS-PHY-SKL-001).

I2 swing (6-mark): see section 10.

G9 handcart [8]

  1. W = F s = 150 N × 10 m (1) = 1500 J (1).
  2. Work done against the resistive forces while pushing = 60 N × 10 m = 600 J (1); energy in the kinetic energy store = 1500 J − 600 J = 900 J (1).
  3. After she lets go, the resistive forces do work until the kinetic energy store is empty: s = W ÷ F = 900 J ÷ 60 N (1) = 15 m (1). ECF from (b).
  4. All 1500 J done by the worker is dissipated by the end, because the cart finishes at rest on level ground, so no energy remains in its kinetic or gravitational potential energy stores (1). It ends in the thermal energy stores of the wheels and axles, the road and the air (the surroundings); check: 60 N × (10 m + 15 m) = 1500 J (1).

Common error: (b) = 1500 J (friction ignored during the push) leading to (c) = 25 m. That is the whole distance, not the roll. Award (c) by ECF (2) but not (b).

10. 6-mark extended response: levels-based mark scheme

Question (S2 I2, Explain, 6 marks, new context): A child sits on a playground swing. An adult pulls the swing back and lets go. The child does not push or pump with her legs. The swing moves back and forth, each swing reaching a slightly lower height than the one before, until it stops. Explain the changes in the way energy is stored, from the moment the swing is released until it stops.

LevelMarksDescriptor
35–6A coherent, logically ordered explanation. Describes the repeated transfer between the gravitational potential energy store (of the child/swing–Earth system) and the kinetic energy store, and explains the decreasing height by work done against air resistance and friction at the pivot, which dissipates energy to thermal energy stores, and links this to conservation (the total is constant; at the end the energy is in the thermal energy store of the surroundings). Store and pathway names are used correctly throughout. 6 marks when the chain is complete and precise; 5 when one link is thin.
23–4Describes the transfer between the gravitational potential and kinetic energy stores in the correct direction for at least one half-swing, and either mentions dissipation to a thermal energy store (or friction / air resistance doing work) or gives a clear, correctly ordered account of a whole swing; the link to the decreasing height or to conservation is missing or unclear. Mostly correct language.
11–2Identifies one or more relevant stores or processes (e.g. “kinetic energy store increases as it swings down”, “friction slows it”) with little or no logical sequence. May use loose language.
00No relevant content.

Rule: an answer that says energy is used up, destroyed or disappears, and never corrects it, cannot go above Level 2. Forms-of-energy names (“heat energy”, “movement energy”) cost precision: place the answer at the bottom of its level.

Indicative content

  • At release the swing is at rest at its highest point: energy is in the gravitational potential energy store of the child/swing–Earth system; the kinetic energy store is empty.
  • Moving down: the gravitational potential energy store decreases and the kinetic energy store of the child and swing increases; energy is transferred mechanically (the weight does work).
  • At the lowest point the kinetic energy store is greatest and the gravitational potential energy store is least.
  • Moving up the other side: the kinetic energy store decreases and the gravitational potential energy store increases, until the swing is momentarily at rest.
  • Air resistance and friction at the pivot (chains, hooks) act against the motion and do work; this transfers energy to the thermal energy stores of the pivot, the swing and the surrounding air. A very small amount is transferred by sound (creaking).
  • So less energy returns to the gravitational potential energy store on each swing, which is why each swing reaches a slightly lower height.
  • When the swing stops, its kinetic energy store is empty and it hangs at the lowest point; the energy it had at release is now in the thermal energy store of the surroundings (dissipated, spread out, not usefully recoverable).
  • The total energy of the closed system (child, swing, Earth, air) is constant throughout; energy is not created or destroyed.
  • (Credit, not required) The energy originally came from the adult: work done pulling the swing back, from the chemical energy store of the adult’s muscles.

Exemplar Level 3 answer (for teacher moderation; do not show before the pupils attempt it)

When the adult lets go, the swing is at rest at its highest point, so the energy is in the gravitational potential energy store of the child–Earth system and the kinetic energy store is empty. As the swing moves down, the child’s weight does work, so energy is transferred from the gravitational potential energy store to the kinetic energy store of the child and swing, which is greatest at the lowest point. As the swing rises again, energy is transferred back from the kinetic energy store to the gravitational potential energy store. On every swing, air resistance and friction at the pivot do work against the motion, so some energy is transferred to the thermal energy stores of the pivot and the surrounding air; it is dissipated. This means less energy returns to the gravitational potential energy store each time, so each swing is a little lower. When the swing stops, all the energy it started with is in the thermal energy store of the surroundings. It has not been destroyed: the total energy of the child, swing, Earth and surrounding air stays the same.

13. Differentiation

Foundation support

  • Give a printed card with the sentence frame and the two lists (8 stores, 4 pathways). Allow it in guided practice only, never in the exit ticket or the mastery check.
  • Session 1 independent: I1–I6 and I7(a). Pupils who finish then attempt I7(b) with the prompt “work done by friction = energy in the kinetic store”.
  • For I4 and I5, ask pupils to complete the GIVEN line with converted units before anything else.
  • Session 2 independent: I1 and the 6-mark response; plan the 6-marker with the prompts “at release / going down / at the bottom / going up / why lower / at the end”. Grade 9 parts (a) and (b) are accessible.
  • Homework: all of Parts A and B are Foundation-accessible; H11 is optional.

Higher stretch

  • Send secure pupils straight to I7 and the Stretch in Session 1 if they finish I3–I6 accurately in under 8 minutes.
  • Grade 9 challenge extension: “If the resistive force were 75 N, how far would the cart roll after she lets go?” (1500 − 75 × 10 = 750 J; 750 ÷ 75 = 10 m.)
  • Sankey extension: “Draw the kettle Sankey for 2.0 kJ supplied at 1 mm per 50 J” (input 40 mm, useful 34 mm, wasted 6 mm).
  • Ask Higher pupils to explain why the gravitational potential energy store belongs to the object–Earth system, not to the object alone.

14. Block 2 mastery check, remediation and escalation

The platform mastery check (Session 2, 40–55 min)

  • A 15-minute programme check on the platform (diagnostic runtime in “programme check” mode). Items are approved mastery-check items tagged to the three concepts: at least 2 per concept, target 3, independent of the teaching examples and practice items.
  • Pupils work alone, no notes, lesson closed. You may read a question aloud; do not explain content. Encourage “Not sure” rather than guessing: a confident wrong answer is recorded as signalling a misconception.
  • The check does not produce a grade. Do not use answers to this lesson’s questions as mastery evidence: they are practice evidence.
  • If the platform says the Block 2 check is unavailable (for example, a concept does not yet have enough approved mastery-check items), do not substitute questions from this lesson or the homework. Record it and tell the programme lead.

Remediation policy (programme manifest)

PassA concept passes the block check when every mastery-check item on it is right and no sure-wrong answer signals one of its misconceptions.
After a first failThe pupil gets the recommended remediation (misconception clinic, prerequisite repair or worked-example re-teach), then a second mastery check on items they have not seen.
After a second failEscalate to the teacher (E1): a 5-minute 1:1 on the evidence; the teacher chooses a live re-teach, a prerequisite mini-lesson, or an override with a reason.
NeverNever “You scored 45%. Next topic.” Never label a pupil from one answer. Never show an untaught concept as insecure.

Which remediation fits which evidence (Block 2)

  • Misconception clinic: sure-wrong answers signalling MIS-PHY-ENE-001, -002, -003, -019 or MIS-PHY-FOR-001. Re-use the matching clinic case with new numbers or a new context; the pupil explains both pupils’ views aloud.
  • Prerequisite repair: work-done errors that are really Toolkit errors (unit conversion, rearranging, standard form). Send to the Block 1 concept, not to W = F s.
  • Worked-example re-teach: a pupil who cannot start a W = F s question, or cannot structure a store-change description. Re-run WE1/WE3 with new numbers, then fade.

Escalation flags (act the same week)

  • A pupil still writes “used up”, “lost” (as a destination), “heat energy” or “electrical energy” after the clinic and in the mastery check.
  • A confident wrong answer on the line-of-action distance (MIS-PHY-FOR-001) after clinic case C4.
  • Toolkit errors in more than one work-done question: the Block 1 skill is not secure; tell the programme lead so the pupil’s pathway includes prerequisite repair.
  • A second failed mastery check on any Block 2 concept (E1: 5-minute 1:1).
  • The platform check is unavailable or a pupil could not complete it (record it; the pupil must not be shown as insecure on an unchecked concept).

15. Physics decisions in this lesson (so you can defend them)

  • Four pathways in this course: mechanically (work done by a force), electrically (work done when a current flows), by heating, by radiation. There is no “particles” pathway.
  • Sound is treated as a mechanical transfer (a vibrating surface does work on the air) and always as a very small amount that also ends in the thermal energy store of the surroundings. Accept “by sound” or “by sound waves” from pupils (creditable; no penalty when listed alongside the four pathways); do not accept “sound energy” as a store.
  • Gravitational potential energy is stored by the object–Earth system; “the gravitational potential energy store of the ball” is accepted at GCSE.
  • Kettle: the useful store is the thermal energy store of the water; the wasted energy goes to the thermal energy stores of the kettle and the surroundings (a little by sound).
  • Braking: friction between the brake pads and discs does the work; the brakes heat up first, then the surroundings.
  • Sankey (Figure 2) is drawn to scale at 1 J = 1.2 units of width: 100 J → 120 units; 85 J → 102 units; 15 J → 18 units; 102 + 18 = 120. Scale bar 24 units = 20 J. Arrowheads do not widen the bands.
  • Number format: a space separates thousands in numbers of five or more digits (86 400 J); four-digit numbers are written without a space (7200 N), as SI allows.
  • “Thermal energy store” is used throughout, never “internal energy store”.
  • Diagram fonts: the SVG labels use the system sans-serif stack (system-ui), not Plus Jakarta Sans as the Inspire Scientific Diagram Standard specifies, because the lesson must not load external fonts. This is a recorded deviation from the standard.
  • Sankey teaching sits in Session 2 guided practice (before G3), not in Session 1 concept construction, so that Session 1 concept construction fits its 15 minutes.
