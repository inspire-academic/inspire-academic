Learning goals

By the end I can…

  1. say what a system is, and name the eight energy stores: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, nuclear, magnetic and electrostatic;
  2. name the four ways energy is transferred (pathways): mechanically, electrically, by heating and by radiation, and never mix up a store with a pathway;
  3. describe all the changes in energy stores for an object projected upwards, a moving object hitting an obstacle, an object accelerated by a constant force, a vehicle slowing down and water brought to the boil in an electric kettle;
  4. explain that energy is never created or destroyed: the total energy of a closed system is constant, and wasted energy is dissipated, usually to the thermal energy store of the surroundings;
  5. read and interpret a Sankey diagram drawn to scale;
  6. calculate work done with W = F s, rearrange it to find F or s, and use the distance moved along the line of action of the force;
  7. explain that work done against friction transfers energy to thermal energy stores (the surfaces warm up).

The calculation routine (use it for every calculation)

  1. 1. GIVEN List every quantity with its symbol and unit. Convert to SI units now (kg, m, s, J, W, N).
  2. 2. FIND Write the symbol of the quantity you need.
  3. 3. RELATIONSHIP Write the equation in symbols (from memory, or from the equation sheet if it is a given equation).
  4. 4. REARRANGE Make the unknown the subject before putting numbers in.
  5. 5. SUBSTITUTE Put the numbers in. This line usually earns the first mark.
  6. 6. SOLVE Calculate. Give the unit, and the significant figures asked for.
  7. 7. CHECK Is the size sensible? Is the unit right for the quantity?

Precise language

Always say: “Energy is transferred from the … store of … to the … store of … by … (mechanically / electrically / by heating / by radiation).”

Name every store in full, for example “the kinetic energy store of the car”, never just “kinetic”.

Never say (these are the mistakes examiners look for): heat energy, movement energy, electrical energy, energy is used up, energy is lost (as a final answer).

If you want to write “lost”, ask yourself: lost to where? The answer is nearly always “dissipated to the thermal energy store of the surroundings”.

1.2 Concept construction

5–20 min · 15 minutes

A system

A system is an object or a group of objects that we choose to study. When a system changes, the way energy is stored in it changes.

Example: a ball thrown upwards. We can choose the system to be the ball and the Earth. As the ball rises, energy moves from one store of this system to another.

A closed system is one where no energy enters or leaves. In a closed system there is no net change to the total energy.

The eight energy stores

A store is where energy is. Always name the store and what it belongs to.

The eight energy stores with an example of each
Energy storeEnergy is in this store when…Example
kinetic energy storean object is movinga bus driving along a road
gravitational potential energy storean object is raised in a gravitational field (the store belongs to the object–Earth system)water held high behind a dam
elastic potential energy storean object is stretched, squashed or benta stretched bungee cord; a squashed spring
thermal energy storean object is warmer (for the same object, a higher temperature means more energy in its thermal energy store)a pot of hot soup
chemical energy storesubstances can take part in chemical reactionsfood; cooking gas and oxygen; a battery
nuclear energy storeenergy is stored in the nuclei of atomsuranium fuel in a nuclear reactor
magnetic energy storemagnets (or magnetic materials) attract or repeltwo magnets held apart
electrostatic energy storeelectric charges attract or repela charged balloon held near a wall

The four pathways

A pathway is how energy is transferred from one store to another. In this course we use four pathways.

The four energy transfer pathways
PathwayWhat happensExample
mechanicallya force does work as an object moves (pushing, pulling, stretching, squashing, a collision)a person pushing a wheelbarrow
electricallywork is done when a current flows in a circuita battery driving a current through a motor
by heatingenergy is transferred from a hotter object to a cooler one because of a temperature differencea hot stove warming a pan
by radiationelectromagnetic waves such as light and infrared carry energysunlight warming a roof
Store or pathway? “Electrically” is a pathway, so there is no electrical energy store. “Heating” is a pathway; the store is the thermal energy store. Sound: a vibrating object pushes on the air and does work on it, so energy carried by sound waves is a mechanical transfer. In answers write “a small amount of energy is transferred by sound to the surroundings”; examiners accept this. Never write “sound energy”: sound is not a store. The energy carried by sound ends up in the thermal energy store of the surroundings.

The sentence to use every time

Energy is transferred from the … store of … to the … store of … by … (mechanically / electrically / by heating / by radiation).

Conservation of energy

Energy can be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated, but it cannot be created or destroyed. The total energy of a closed system is constant.

Dissipation (“wasted” energy)

In every real change, some energy is transferred to stores where it is less useful. We say the energy is dissipated. It usually ends up in the thermal energy store of the surroundings: it spreads out, warming the surroundings by a very small amount, so it is very hard to use again. It has not been destroyed.

Work done: energy transferred by a force

When a force moves an object, the force does work. Work done is energy transferred.

W = F s   work done (J) = force (N) × distance (m)

  • s is the distance moved along the line of action of the force, that is, in the direction the force acts. A distance moved at right angles to the force does not count.
  • 1 J = 1 N m: one joule of work is done when a force of 1 N moves an object 1 m along the line of action of the force.
  • Work done against friction causes heating. Rub your hands together: you do work against friction, energy is transferred to the thermal energy stores of your hands, and they get warmer.

You must remember W = F s: it is not on the exam equation sheet.

1.3 Worked examples

20–30 min · 10 minutes

Examples 1 and 3 are fully worked. In examples 2 and 4 you finish the working, then check.

Example 1 (fully worked): describing store changes

A battery-powered toy car starts from rest and speeds up along a level floor. Describe the changes in the way energy is stored.

  1. System: the toy car, including its battery and motor.
  2. Start: energy is in the chemical energy store of the battery. The car is at rest, so its kinetic energy store is empty.
  3. Transfer 1: energy is transferred electrically (by the current) from the chemical energy store of the battery to the motor.
  4. Transfer 2: the motor’s force does work on the wheels, so energy is transferred mechanically to the kinetic energy store of the car, which increases as the car speeds up.
  5. Dissipation: some energy is transferred by heating to the thermal energy stores of the motor, the axles and the surroundings (from the current in the motor and from friction). A very small amount is transferred by sound.
  6. Conservation: decrease in the chemical energy store = increase in the kinetic energy store + energy dissipated. The total energy of the car and its surroundings is unchanged.

Example 2 (faded): you finish it

A coconut falls from a palm tree, hits the sand and stops. Air resistance is very small. Describe the changes in the way energy is stored.

  1. Before it falls: energy is in the gravitational potential energy store of the coconut–Earth system.
  2. As it falls: energy is transferred from the ______ store to the ______ store, mechanically, because the coconut’s weight does work on it.
  3. When it hits the sand: the kinetic energy store of the coconut decreases to zero. The coconut does work pushing the sand aside, so energy is transferred mechanically to ______.
  4. Conservation: ______.
Check the model answer (after you have written yours)

2. From the gravitational potential energy store of the coconut–Earth system to the kinetic energy store of the coconut.

3. The thermal energy stores of the coconut, the sand and the surroundings (the energy is dissipated). A very small amount is transferred by sound.

4. The total energy of the coconut, Earth, sand and air is the same before and after: energy has been transferred, not destroyed.

Example 3 (fully worked): calculating work done

A market trader drags a sack of yams 8.0 m across a level concrete floor. She pulls with a steady horizontal force of 150 N, and the sack moves at a steady speed. Calculate the work done by her pulling force, and say where the energy goes.

GIVEN F = 150 N; s = 8.0 m (horizontal, the same direction as the force). Both already in SI units.

FIND W

RELATIONSHIP W = F s

REARRANGE not needed: W is already the subject.

SUBSTITUTE W = 150 N × 8.0 m

SOLVE W = 1200 J

CHECK N × m = N m = J, so the unit is right. 1200 J is a sensible amount for dragging a heavy sack across a room.

Where the energy goes: the sack moves at a steady speed, so its kinetic energy store does not change. The work is done against friction, so 1200 J is transferred to the thermal energy stores of the sack, the floor and the surroundings: the underside of the sack and the floor warm up slightly. The energy is dissipated.

Example 4 (faded): rearranging W = F s

The cable of a lift in a block of flats pulls upwards on the lift with a steady force of 7200 N. The work done by this force is 86 400 J. Calculate how far the lift rises.

GIVEN F = 7200 N; W = 86 400 J

FIND s

RELATIONSHIP W = F s

REARRANGE s = ______

SUBSTITUTE s = ______ ÷ ______

SOLVE s = ______ m

CHECK ______

Check the model answer (after you have written yours)

REARRANGE: s = W ÷ F. SUBSTITUTE: s = 86 400 J ÷ 7200 N. SOLVE: s = 12 m.

CHECK: J ÷ N = N m ÷ N = m, so the unit is right. 12 m is about four floors of a block of flats, which is sensible.

2.2 Misconception clinic

5–15 min · 10 minutes

Two pupils disagree. Decide who is right and explain why. Your teacher starts with the case the class found hardest in the exit ticket.

C1 A phone battery is flat at the end of the day. [2 marks]

Pupil A says: “The energy in the battery has been used up. It has gone.”
Pupil B says: “The energy has been transferred from the chemical energy store of the battery. Most of it is now in the thermal energy store of the surroundings.”

C2 An electric kettle boils some water. [2 marks]

Pupil A says: “Energy is transferred electrically to the heating element, then by heating to the thermal energy store of the water.”
Pupil B says: “The kettle changes electrical energy into heat energy.”

C3 A skateboard rolls along level ground and slows to a stop. [2 marks]

Pupil A says: “Friction destroys the kinetic energy. That is why the skateboard stops.”
Pupil B says: “Friction does work on the skateboard. This transfers energy from its kinetic energy store to the thermal energy stores of the wheels, the ground and the air.”

C4 A porter carries a load of weight 200 N on her head. She walks 50 m along a level path, and the load stays at the same height. Her head pushes up on the load with a force of 200 N. [2 marks]

Pupil A says: “The upward force does no work on the load during the walk, because the load does not move up or down.”
Pupil B says: “The work done by the upward force on the load is 200 N × 50 m = 10 000 J.”

2.3 Guided practice: less familiar contexts

15–25 min · 10 minutes

Try each one yourself first, then check.

Check the model answer (after you have written yours)

The gravitational potential energy store of the jumper–Earth system decreases all the way down.

While she falls freely, energy is transferred mechanically (her weight does work) to her kinetic energy store, which increases.

Once the cord is tight it starts to stretch, and energy is also transferred to the elastic potential energy store of the cord. At first her weight is bigger than the pull of the cord, so she keeps speeding up and her kinetic energy store still increases. When the cord pulls up more strongly than her weight, she slows down: her kinetic energy store decreases while the elastic potential energy store of the cord keeps increasing. At the lowest point her kinetic energy store is empty and the elastic potential energy store of the cord is at its largest.

Throughout, some energy is dissipated by air resistance to the thermal energy store of the surroundings. At the lowest point: decrease in the gravitational potential energy store = increase in the elastic potential energy store of the cord + energy dissipated.

G2 A toy car is released from rest at the top of a curved track. Between the top and the bottom, the energy in the gravitational potential energy store of the car–Earth system decreases by 2.4 J. At the bottom there is 1.9 J in the kinetic energy store of the car.

Hint: the energy dissipated is the work done against the resistive force; W = F s.

Check the model answer (after you have written yours)

(a) 2.4 J − 1.9 J = 0.5 J dissipated (to the thermal energy stores of the car, the track and the air).

(b) W = F s, so F = W ÷ s = 0.5 J ÷ 2.0 m = 0.25 N. The resistive force acts along the track, so the distance along the track is the right distance.

Sankey diagrams (read this with your teacher before G3)

A Sankey diagram shows energy transfers as arrows. The width of each arrow is proportional to the energy. The output arrows together are exactly as wide as the input arrow, because energy is conserved.

G3 The motor of a sewing machine is supplied with 250 J electrically. 180 J is transferred usefully to the kinetic energy store of the needle and its moving parts. The rest is dissipated.

Check the model answer (after you have written yours)

(a) 250 J − 180 J = 70 J.

(b) Input: 250 ÷ 5 = 50 mm. Useful: 180 ÷ 5 = 36 mm. Wasted: 70 ÷ 5 = 14 mm. Check: 36 mm + 14 mm = 50 mm, the width of the input arrow.
