1. Purpose and objectives

Block intent (programme manifest): repair the quantitative skills every Energy calculation depends on, so later errors can be diagnosed as physics errors rather than maths errors. The block covers four approved concepts. It is not a complete maths course: each tool is taught only as far as Energy calculations need it.

Misconceptions this block targets: MIS-PHY-SKL-001 to MIS-PHY-SKL-006 (section 8).

2. Prerequisites and materials

Prerequisites: the four Toolkit concepts have no prerequisite concepts in the curriculum graph. Pupils need KS3 arithmetic and: times tables and multiplying/dividing by 10, 100 and 1000; the idea of an equation with symbols; speed = distance ÷ time (KS3); plotting and reading points on a grid. Check the baseline programme check first: a pupil who is secure on a tool skips that part (see Differentiation).

Materials:

  • Scientific calculator for every pupil (check each has a ×10x or EXP key and a (−) key; a class model on the board if possible).
  • Mini-whiteboards and pens (retrieval and hinge questions).
  • A ruler per pupil for graph reading (drawing the reading lines and the gradient triangle on paper or on screen).
  • Device access to ISM Class for the pupil lesson; platform access for the Block 1 mastery check in Session 2.
  • Optional: a printed copy of Graphs 1–3 per pupil so they can draw on them. The pupil page prints cleanly (model answers are hidden in print).

Energy equations as vehicles. This block uses P = E/t, Ek = ½mv², Ep = mgh, W = Fs (recall equations) and ΔE = mcΔθ, Ee = ½ke² (given on the AQA equation sheet) only as material for converting, substituting and rearranging. In practice questions, recall equations are printed and labelled “Hint — recall equation”; given equations are printed as on the equation sheet. Do not teach the physics of these equations yet: it comes in Blocks 2–5. Use the store language (“energy is transferred to the thermal energy store of the water”) and never say energy is “used up” or “lost”.

3. The calculation routine

One routine for the whole programme (programme manifest, calculationRoutine, status: proposed, pending approval). Every worked example on the pupil page is set out in these seven steps. Insist on it for every question this week: it is the scaffold for Blocks 2–5.

#StepWhat pupils say / do
1GIVENList every quantity with its symbol and unit. Convert to SI units now (kg, m, s, J, W).
2FINDWrite the symbol of the quantity you need.
3RELATIONSHIPWrite the equation in symbols (from memory, or from the equation sheet if it is a given equation).
4REARRANGEMake the unknown the subject before putting numbers in.
5SUBSTITUTEPut the numbers in. This line usually earns the first mark.
6SOLVECalculate. Give the unit, and the significant figures asked for.
7CHECKIs the size sensible? Is the unit right for the quantity?

SUBSTITUTE usually earns the first mark. In AQA “calculate” questions, the first marking point is normally the correct substitution (or the correct rearrangement with numbers in), not the equation alone. A pupil who writes the substituted line gets that mark even if the arithmetic goes wrong; a pupil who writes only the final number and gets it wrong scores nothing. Tell pupils this explicitly, and mark their practice this way.

Run every hinge with pupil screens down: read the question aloud and show it on the board. The worked answer to Hinge 2 is on the pupil page (Tool 3).

All pupils show their answer on mini-whiteboards at the same moment. Decide in 30 seconds: if most are right, move on and see the few who are not during guided practice; if most are wrong, stop and use the route for the most common wrong answer.

7. Answers, worked solutions and mark schemes

Every pupil question, in the order it appears on the pupil page. Marks are shown in square brackets. Mark schemes give one mark per marking point; allow error carried forward from an earlier step unless stated. Accept equivalent answers (for example 2 400 000 J for 2.4 × 106 J) unless the question asks for a particular form or unit. Where a question asks for a unit, a wrong or missing unit loses the final mark only: for example “50” with correct working scores 2 of 3 on I7, and a unitless answer scores at most 1 of 2 on a 2-mark question that requires a unit. A correct final answer with no working earns full marks unless the question asks for working.

The graphs (copies for reference)

The pupil captions give only the axis ranges, so that reading questions still test reading. Full data: Graph 1 straight line from (0 s, 20 °C) to (300 s, 50 °C), plotted points at (0, 20), (100, 30), (200, 40), (300, 50); one small square = 10 s × 2 °C; the worked reading (60 s, 26 °C) and the gradient triangle (100 s, 30 °C)–(300 s, 50 °C) are drawn for Example 7. Graph 3 straight line from (0 min, 0 kJ) to (10 min, 300 kJ), points every 2 min; one small square = 0.5 min × 10 kJ. Think 1 A pupil converts 3.0 km into metres and gets 0.003 m. Without calculating, how can you tell this is wrong? What is the right answer? fields: b1_s1_cc1

Answer: A metre is a smaller unit than a kilometre, so there must be more metres than kilometres: the number must get bigger, not smaller. 3.0 km = 3.0 × 1000 = 3000 m.

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-001

Think 2 Why is 58 × 105 J not written in standard form? Write it correctly. fields: b1_s1_cc2

Answer: The first number must be at least 1 and less than 10; 58 is not. 58 × 105 J = 5.8 × 106 J (5 800 000 J).

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-003

Think 3 Rearrange W = Fs to make s the subject. Say what you did to both sides. fields: b1_s1_cc3

Answer: s = W ÷ F. Both sides were divided by F (s was multiplied by F, so divide to undo it).

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-004

Think 4 Look at Graph 2 below (the trotro). What is one small square worth on the time axis? On the distance axis? fields: b1_s1_cc4

I7 [3] An iron cooking pot has a mass of 1.2 kg. The specific heat capacity of iron is 450 J/kg °C. 27 kJ of energy is transferred to the thermal energy store of the pot. Calculate the rise in temperature of the pot. fields: b1_s1_i7

Answer: ΔE = 27 kJ = 27 000 J. Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c) = 27 000 ÷ (1.2 × 450) = 27 000 ÷ 540 = 50 °C.

Mark scheme (1 mark per point):
  1. 27 000 J
  2. Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c) with substitution 27 000 ÷ (1.2 × 450)
  3. 50 °C, unit required: “50” with no unit loses this mark (2 of 3)

Case 1 The question: Convert 350 g into kilograms. fields: b1_s2_mc1

Answer: Pupil A. A kilogram is a bigger unit than a gram, so there are fewer kilograms: divide by 1000. Pupil B converted in the wrong direction; 350 000 kg would be heavier than several lorries.

Case 2 The question: A 1.2 kW heater is switched on for 5 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. fields: b1_s2_mc2

Answer: Pupil B. Every quantity must be in SI units before substituting: 1.2 kW = 1200 W and 5 minutes = 300 s. Pupil A substituted kW and minutes, so the answer is 60 000 times too small.

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-002

Case 3 The question: A 40 W fan transfers 2400 J of energy. Calculate how long it runs. (Hint: P = E ÷ t.) fields: b1_s2_mc3

Answer: Pupil B. Multiply both sides by t, then divide by P: t = E ÷ P. Unit check: J ÷ (J/s) = s. Pupil A put the equation the wrong way up; 0.017 s is absurdly short for a fan.

Case 4 The question: A rugby player of mass 80 kg has 3240 J in their kinetic energy store. Calculate their speed. (Hint: Ek = ½mv².) fields: b1_s2_mc4

Answer: Pupil B. Speed is the gradient of a distance–time graph (change in distance ÷ change in time). Pupil A read a value (a distance, 300 m) and gave it a speed unit.

I11 [4] Use Graph 2 (the trotro). (a) Calculate the average speed of the trotro for the whole 60 s. [2 marks] (b) A pupil says the average speed is (10 + 12) ÷ 2 = 11 m/s. Explain why this is wrong. [2 marks] fields: b1_s2_i3a, b1_s2_i3b

Answer: (a) average speed = total distance ÷ total time = 540 m ÷ 60 s = 9.0 m/s. (b) Average speed is total distance ÷ total time. The trotro spent different times at each speed (30 s at 10 m/s, 20 s at 12 m/s) and was stationary for 10 s (section B); the mean of the two speeds ignores the times.

Mark scheme (1 mark per point):
  1. (a) 540 ÷ 60
  2. (a) 9.0 m/s
  3. (b) average speed = total distance ÷ total time
  4. (b) it spends different times at each speed, including 10 s stationary in B, so the mean of 10 and 12 m/s is not the average speed

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-006

Grade 9 [7] A school kitchen in Accra heats 25 kg of water in a large pot on a gas burner, from 22 °C to 100 °C. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/kg °C. Assume energy is transferred to the thermal energy store of the water at a steady rate of 6.5 kW.
(a) Calculate the time taken to heat the water, in minutes.
(b) In practice it takes longer than your answer to (a). Explain why. fields: b1_s2_g9a, b1_s2_g9b

Answer: (a) Δθ = 100 − 22 = 78 °C. ΔE = m c Δθ = 25 × 4200 × 78 = 8 190 000 J (8.19 × 106 J). P = 6.5 kW = 6500 W. P = E ÷ t, so t = E ÷ P = 8 190 000 ÷ 6500 = 1260 s = 1260 ÷ 60 = 21 minutes. (b) Some energy is transferred by heating to the thermal energy store of the pot and is dissipated to the surroundings, so energy is transferred to the water at less than 6.5 kW; it takes longer to transfer 8.19 MJ to the water.

Mark scheme (1 mark per point):
  1. (a) Δθ = 78 °C used in ΔE = 25 × 4200 × 78
  2. (a) ΔE = 8 190 000 J (8.19 × 106 J)
  3. (a) P = 6500 W and t = E ÷ P (rearranged)
  4. (a) t = 1260 s
  5. (a) 21 minutes (allow error carried forward from a wrong ΔE for the last three marks)
  6. (b) energy is transferred to the pot / dissipated to the surroundings (thermal energy store of the surroundings); also accept: the water itself transfers energy to the surroundings, faster as it gets hotter; some water evaporates
  7. (b) so the rate of energy transfer to the water is less than 6.5 kW (less than 6500 J each second), so a longer time is needed

Watch for: MIS-PHY-SKL-002 (6.5 not 6500: 1 260 000 s), MIS-PHY-SKL-004 (E × P), “lost” / “used up” in (b): no mark for (b) first point unless energy is said to go somewhere

9. Differentiation

Foundation support

  • Square-root rearrangements are Higher demand (band 3): Hinge 2, I8, GP2 and Clinic Case 4 are tagged “Higher”. Foundation pupils do the rearrangements without a root instead (Example 5, E2) and may omit GP2; they should still see Case 4 as a discussion.
  • Give a printed routine card and a printed prefix table; allow pupils to annotate GIVEN with ↑ / ↓ arrows for the direction of each conversion.
  • For graphs, give the “one small square” sentence frame: “___ across ___ small squares, so one small square is ___”.
  • Grade 9 challenge prompts, one at a time only if stuck: (1) “Find ΔE first: what is Δθ?” (2) “Now you know E and P: which equation links energy, power and time?” (3) “Your time is in seconds: the question wants minutes.”
  • Reduce, do not change: Band 1 and Band 2 of the independent practice are enough for a Foundation pupil in Session 1 if the stretch is not reached; record that I8 was not attempted rather than marking it wrong.

Higher stretch

  • Pupils secure on the baseline start at Band 2 and must reach I8 and the Grade 9 challenge.
  • Extension prompts: “Show that v = √(2Ek/m) has the unit m/s (J = kg m²/s²).” “In GP1, what would the graph look like if clouds covered the sun for 2 minutes? What would the gradient be then?” “In the Grade 9 challenge, estimate the actual rate of energy transfer to the water if it really took 30 minutes.” (8 190 000 J ÷ 1800 s = 4550 W.)
  • Ask Higher pupils to write the CHECK line as an estimate before they calculate (MS 1d).

11. Escalation flags: when to stop and see a pupil 1:1

  • The pupil gets both exit-ticket conversions (E1) wrong in the same way as in retrieval (R2 or R4): a stable direction error, not a slip.
  • The pupil cannot say what one small square is worth on Graph 2 after the concept construction (Think 4 wrong, or G4 misread): check reading of scales and number sense before any more graph work.
  • Arithmetic, not physics, is the barrier: the pupil cannot multiply or divide by 1000 or 60 reliably, or cannot use a calculator’s basic functions. This is below the Toolkit; refer to maths support and tell the programme lead.
  • The pupil does not write working even when prompted twice, or copies model answers into the boxes before attempting (the platform saves every field; look for identical wording).
  • A second mastery-check fail on the same concept (policy E1): 5-minute 1:1 on the evidence, then a live re-teach, a prerequisite mini-lesson, or an override with a reason.
  • Any sign of distress or disengagement around maths (e.g. blank exit ticket, “I’m just bad at maths”): talk privately; do not record it as a mastery failure.
