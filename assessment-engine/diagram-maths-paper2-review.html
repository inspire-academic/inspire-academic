Properties of shapesAngles in polygons

What is the sum of the interior angles of a regular octagon (8 sides)?

A 1440°
B 360°
C 1080°
D 900°
E Not sure
Correct: C — the sum of interior angles of a polygon is (n−2)×180°; for an octagon (n=8), this gives (8−2)×180=1080°.
Properties of shapesCircle theorems (Higher)

A tangent touches a circle at point A. The radius OA is drawn to the point of contact. What is the angle between the tangent and the radius OA?

A 45°
B 60°
C 180°
D 90°
E Not sure
Correct: D — a tangent to a circle is always perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact; this angle is always exactly 90°.
Properties of shapesCongruence and similarity

Two rectangles are similar. The smaller rectangle has an area of 8 cm². The larger rectangle has sides exactly 3 times the length of the smaller rectangle's corresponding sides. What is the area of the larger rectangle?

A 24 cm²
B 72 cm²
C 216 cm²
D 11 cm²
E Not sure
Correct: B — area scales by the square of the linear scale factor: 3²=9, so the larger area is 8×9=72 cm².

A (−3, 2)

Correct: D — for a 90° clockwise rotation about the origin, the rule is (x,y)→(y,−x); applying this to (2,3) gives (3,−2).
Perimeter, area, volumeArea of 2D shapes

A trapezium has parallel sides of length 6 cm and 10 cm, and a perpendicular height of 4 cm. What is its area?

A 64 cm²
B 32 cm²
C 12 cm²
D 20 cm²

Correct: B — Area of a trapezium = ½×(sum of parallel sides)×height = ½×(6+10)×4 = ½×16×4 = 32 cm².

Angles and geometryParallel lines

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle formed is 115°, and a second angle lies between the parallel lines, on the SAME side of the transversal (co-interior/allied angles). What is the size of the second angle?

A 115°

D 65°
E Not sure
Correct: D — co-interior (allied) angles between parallel lines always sum to 180°, so the second angle is 180−115=65°.
Angles and geometryAngles in polygons

A regular nonagon (9 sides) has all exterior angles equal. What is the size of each exterior angle?

A 40°
B 140°
C 360°
D 45°
E Not sure
Correct: A — exterior angles of ANY polygon always sum to 360°, so for a regular nonagon (9 equal exterior angles), each one is 360÷9=40°.
Angles and geometryBearings

From a lighthouse L, the bearing of a ship A is 040°, and the bearing of a second ship B is 115°. What is the angle ALB (the angle between LA and LB, as seen from the lighthouse)?

A 75°
B 155°
C 40°
D 115°
E Not sure
Correct: A — the angle between the two bearings, both measured from the same point, is simply the difference between them: 115°−40°=75°.

Two straight lines cross at a point. One of the four angles formed is 55°. What is the size of the angle vertically opposite to it?

A 125°

Correct: B — vertically opposite angles (formed when two straight lines cross) are always equal, so the angle vertically opposite the 55° angle is also 55°.
Constructions and lociAngle bisector

A dog is kept in a garden corner where two straight fences meet at a right angle. The dog always stays at an equal distance from both fences. What construction describes the dog's possible path?

A A circle centred at the corner
B A line parallel to one fence
C The angle bisector of the angle between the two fences
D The perpendicular bisector of the distance between the fences
E Not sure
Correct: C — the locus of points equidistant from two lines meeting at a point is the angle bisector of the angle between them; it splits the angle exactly in half.
Constructions and lociPerpendicular bisector

Points A and B are marked on a map, 8 cm apart. A treasure is buried at a point equidistant from both A and B. Which construction identifies every possible location for the treasure?

A The perpendicular bisector of AB
B The angle bisector at A
C A circle of radius 4 cm centred at the midpoint of AB
D A straight line through A and B
E Not sure
Correct: A — every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is exactly the same distance from A as it is from B; it's constructed with a compass by finding two intersecting arcs from A and B, then joining the intersection points.
Constructions and lociAngle bisector

Two paths meet at a point, forming an angle. A new path is being planned that bisects this angle exactly in half. Which piece of equipment is essential for constructing this bisector accurately (not by eye)?

A A compass, to draw intersecting arcs from each arm of the angle
B A protractor, to measure exactly half the angle
C A ruler alone, to judge the halfway line by eye
D A set square, to construct a right angle
E Not sure
Correct: A — an angle bisector is constructed with a compass: draw an arc crossing both arms of the angle, then from each crossing point draw a second arc of equal radius; the line from the vertex through where these arcs intersect is the exact bisector.
Constructions and lociConstructing a triangle

A student wants to construct a 60° angle using only a compass and straightedge, without using a protractor. Which construction achieves this?

A Bisect a 90° angle constructed with a set square
B Draw any triangle and measure one angle
C Construct an equilateral triangle — each of its angles is exactly 60°
D Draw a circle and divide its circumference into 5 equal arcs
E Not sure
Correct: C — an equilateral triangle (constructed with a compass by drawing two equal-radius arcs from each end of a line segment) has all three sides equal, which means all three angles are exactly 60° each.
TrigonometryPythagoras' theorem

A right-angled triangle has two shorter sides of 9 cm and 12 cm. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

A 15 cm
B 21 cm
C 225 cm
D 10.5 cm
E Not sure
Correct: A — Pythagoras' theorem: hypotenuse²=9²+12²=81+144=225, so hypotenuse=√225=15 cm.
TrigonometrySOH CAH TOA

In a right-angled triangle, an angle is 40°, and the side ADJACENT to this angle is 10 cm. What is the length of the hypotenuse? (Use cos 40° ≈ 0.77)

A 7.7 cm
B 10.77 cm
C 9.23 cm
D 13.0 cm
E Not sure
Correct: D — CAH says cos(angle)=adjacent÷hypotenuse, so hypotenuse=adjacent÷cos(angle)=10÷0.77≈13.0 cm.
TrigonometrySOH CAH TOA

A person stands 20 m from the base of a tower. The angle of elevation to the top of the tower is 35°. Using tan(35°) ≈ 0.70, what is the height of the tower, to 1 decimal place?

A 28.6 m
B 14.0 m
C 20.7 m
D 19.3 m
E Not sure
Correct: B — TOA: tan(angle)=opposite÷adjacent, so height=adjacent×tan(angle)=20×0.70=14.0 m.

What is the exact value of tan(45°)?

A 1

D 1/2
E Not sure
Correct: A — tan(45°) is one of the standard exact trig values: tan(45°)=1, since sin(45°)=cos(45°)=√2/2, and their ratio is 1.

VectorsVector notation

Vector p = (4, 6). Which of the following vectors is PARALLEL to p?

A (6, 4)
B (4, 7)
C (2, 3)
D (−4, 6)
E Not sure
Correct: C — (2,3) = ½ × (4,6), so it's a scalar multiple of p — every component is scaled by the same factor (½), which is exactly what makes two vectors parallel.
VectorsVector notation

Point P has position vector p = (2, 5) and point Q has position vector q = (6, 1). What is the vector PQ (from P to Q)?

A (4, −4)
B (8, 6)
C (−4, 4)
D (4, 4)
E Not sure
Correct: A — the vector from P to Q is found by (position of Q) − (position of P) = q−p = (6−2, 1−5) = (4,−4).

ProbabilityTree diagrams

A spinner has 3 equal red sections and 1 equal blue section. It is spun twice. What is the probability of getting red both times?

A 3/4
B 9/16
C 1/2
D 3/16
E Not sure
Correct: B — each spin is independent with P(red)=3/4, so P(red, then red) = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16.
ProbabilityVenn diagrams

In a survey of 40 students, 25 play football, 18 play basketball, and 10 play both sports. How many students play NEITHER sport?

A 15
B 7
C 33
D 3
E Not sure
Correct: B — students playing at least one sport = 25+18−10=33 (adding both totals then subtracting the overlap once, since it's counted twice). Students playing neither = 40−33=7.
ProbabilityVenn diagrams

A card is drawn from a standard pack. Let event A = "drawing a King" and event B = "drawing a Queen". Are events A and B mutually exclusive?

A Yes — a single card cannot be both a King and a Queen at the same time
B No — some cards are both Kings and Queens
C Yes — because P(A) and P(B) are equal
D No — because A and B are independent events
E Not sure
Correct: A — mutually exclusive events cannot happen at the same time; since a single card can never be both a King AND a Queen simultaneously, these two events are mutually exclusive.
ProbabilityTree diagrams

A 1/2

Correct: D — for two independent events, P(both) = P(first) × P(second) = 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8.

StatisticsCumulative frequency (Higher)

A cumulative frequency graph for 80 students is drawn, and the curve passes through the point (55, 40). What does this point represent?

A The mean mark
B The range of marks
C The median mark
D The modal mark
E Not sure
Correct: C — since cumulative frequency 40 is exactly half of the total (80), the mark value at that point (55) is the MEDIAN: the middle value once all the data is arranged in order.
