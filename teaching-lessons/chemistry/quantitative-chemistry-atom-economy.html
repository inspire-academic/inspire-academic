3Core Lesson

1. Desired product and by-products

Atoms are conserved, but a reaction can make more than one product. The desired product is the substance we want. Other products are by-products. Atom economy measures the proportion of starting-material mass built into the desired product.

percentage atom economy = total Mr of desired product / total Mr of all reactants × 100

2. Four calculation rules

  1. Use the balanced equation.
  2. Multiply every Mr by its coefficient.
  3. Put only the desired product in the numerator.
  4. Include every reactant in the denominator.

Equation property: better filtration may improve percentage yield, but it cannot change atom economy for a fixed balanced equation.

3. Choosing a route Higher

A high atom economy is valuable, but industry may also consider yield, rate, equilibrium position, energy/cost and whether by-products are useful. Compare every supplied criterion before making a supported judgement.

Build the reactant total accurately

TiO21 × 80 = 80+2Mg2 × 24 = 48→all reactants80 + 48 = 128

Desired Ti = 48: 48 ÷ 128 × 100 = 37.5%.

The written 2 doubles magnesium's mass contribution; it does not change magnesium's Ar.

Atom economy

Desired product ÷ all reactants

Fixed by the balanced equation. It asks: where do the atoms go?

Percentage yield

Actual product ÷ theoretical product

Affected by practical recovery. It asks: how much did we collect?

Do not swap the measures. A reaction can have high atom economy but low experimental yield, or vice versa.

5Worked Examples

Example 1 — calcium oxide

Equation
CaCO3 → CaO + CO2; desired = CaO.
Desired total Mr
1 × 56 = 56.
All reactants
1 × 100 = 100.
Answer
56 ÷ 100 × 100 = 56%.

Example 2 — titanium

Equation
TiO2 + 2Mg → Ti + 2MgO; desired = Ti.
Desired total Mr
1 × 48 = 48.
All reactants
80 + (2 × 24) = 128.
Answer
48 ÷ 128 × 100 = 37.5%.

Example 3 — the only product

Equation
2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.
Reasoning
Water is the only product, so all reactant atoms enter it.
Answer
100% atom economy.

6Misconception Clinic

“Atom economy and yield are the same.”
Atom economy is fixed by the equation. Yield compares actual with theoretical product in a practical process.
“Use only the largest reactant in the denominator.”
Include coefficient × Mr for every reactant.
“Coefficients change Mᵣ.”
A coefficient multiplies the mass contribution; it never changes a substance's formula or Mr.
“A by-product is always useless.”
Some by-products have value. Their usefulness may affect route choice, even though they are not the desired product in this calculation.
“Better apparatus increases atom economy.”
It may reduce practical loss and improve yield. Atom economy changes only if the reaction equation or desired product changes.
“The highest atom economy always wins.”
Route choice may also depend on yield, rate, equilibrium, energy/cost and useful by-products.
Higher atom economy means less mass becomes by-product, but other supplied practical factors can still matter.
