3Core Lesson

Before you titrate

Wear eye protection. Acids and alkalis may be irritant or corrosive. Use a pipette filler—never pipette by mouth—and rinse splashes with plenty of water.

  1. Rinse the burette with the solution it will contain, fill it below eye level, run solution through the jet, check for air bubbles, then remove the funnel.
  2. Rinse the volumetric pipette with the solution it will deliver. Use it with a pipette filler to transfer a fixed aliquot into a conical flask.
  3. Add only two or three drops of a suitable indicator and place the flask on a white tile. Phenolphthalein or methyl orange is suitable for a strong acid–strong alkali titration; universal indicator has a broad colour change and is not suitable for an accurate endpoint.

Find the endpoint accurately

  1. Record the initial burette reading from the bottom of the meniscus at eye level, to two decimal places.
  2. Do one rough titration to locate the endpoint.
  3. Repeat. Swirl continuously; near the endpoint, add titrant one drop at a time and rinse flask walls with distilled water.
  4. Stop at the first faint permanent colour change. Record the final reading to two decimal places and calculate titre = final − initial.
  5. Repeat until accurate titres are concordant. Use the tolerance stated in the question or centre method; 0.10 cm³ is a common exam criterion.

5Worked Examples

Example 1 — explain the accuracy choices

Remove funnel
Otherwise unnoticed drops may enter after the initial reading and make the delivered volume unreliable.
White tile
Provides a neutral background so the first faint colour change is easier to judge.
Wash flask walls
Distilled water moves splashes into the mixture but adds no reacting moles, so it does not change the endpoint volume.
Eye-level meniscus
Avoids parallax; for a colourless solution, read the bottom of the meniscus.

Example 2 — unknown concentration Higher

23.60 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ HCl neutralises 25.00 cm³ NaOH.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Convert known V
23.60 cm³ = 0.02360 dm³.
Known moles
0.100 × 0.02360 = 0.002360 mol HCl.
Ratio
1:1, so NaOH moles = 0.002360 mol.
Convert unknown V
25.00 cm³ = 0.02500 dm³.
Unknown c
0.002360 ÷ 0.02500 = 0.0944 mol/dm³.

Non-1:1 warning: use the balanced equation. For H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O, NaOH moles are twice H₂SO₄ moles.

6Misconception Clinic

“The rough titre belongs in the mean.”

No. It locates the endpoint; the mean uses concordant accurate titres.

“Adding distilled water changes the titre.”

Not when it only washes the flask walls: reacting moles are unchanged.

“Every reaction is 1:1.”

Coefficients set the mole ratio. Never transfer moles until the equation is balanced.

“A strong pink endpoint is safer.”

A strong colour usually means too much titrant was added. Aim for the first faint permanent change.
