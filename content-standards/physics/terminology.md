# Terminology

Language every Inspire Physics item uses, and language it must not. Items teach by example: a loose phrase in a stem teaches the misconception.

## Energy

| Use | Never use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| energy is **transferred** (between stores / by a pathway) | energy is "used up", "lost", "made", "produced", "turned into" (in stems and correct answers) | Conservation: energy is never destroyed or created. "Wasted" and "dissipated" are acceptable for energy transferred to less useful stores. |
| **kinetic energy store**, **gravitational potential energy store**, **elastic potential energy store**, **thermal energy store**, **chemical energy store** | "movement energy", "heat energy" as a store name | AQA uses the store language. "Heat" is not a store. |
| energy transferred **by heating**, **mechanically** (by a force doing work), **electrically** (by a current) | "heat flows" as the only description | AQA pathways. |
| **temperature** (°C) vs **thermal energy** (J) | using them as synonyms | Specific heat capacity items depend on this distinction. |
| **power** = rate of energy transfer (W) | power as "amount of energy" or "strength" | Power/energy confusion is a core misconception. |
| **efficiency** as a decimal or a percentage, never above 1 (100%) | efficiency with a unit, or efficiency "of energy" | Efficiency is a ratio. |
| **dissipated** / **wasted** energy, usually to the thermal store of the surroundings | "destroyed", "disappears" | Conservation. |
| **renewable** = being, or can be, replenished as it is used | "never runs out", "free", "clean" as the definition | Precise AQA definition. |
| **closed system** | "isolated" without explanation | AQA term. |

## General

- **Mass** (kg) and **weight** (N) are never interchanged.
- **Speed** vs **velocity**: velocity only when direction matters.
- "Calculate" questions show units in the working; answers carry units unless the unit is given.
- Quantities are written with the AQA symbol and unit at first use in an item: "gravitational field strength, g = 9.8 N/kg".
- Everyday contexts are welcome (including African contexts), but the physics wording is exact.
- Reading level: short sentences; one idea per sentence; no double negatives; avoid "which of the following is NOT" unless the item's purpose is negation, and then write **not** in bold.
