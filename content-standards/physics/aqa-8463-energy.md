# AQA GCSE Physics 8463 — 4.1 Energy

Summarised from the specification (v1.1, 30 Sep 2019). Statement numbers are AQA's. The whole of 4.1 is in both separate Physics (8463) and Combined Science Trilogy (8464) **except Required practical 2, which is separate Physics only**.

## 4.1.1 Energy changes in a system

**4.1.1.1 Energy stores and systems**
- A system is an object or group of objects. When a system changes, the way energy is stored changes.
- Describe all the changes in the way energy is stored for common situations: an object projected upwards; a moving object hitting an obstacle; an object accelerated by a constant force; a vehicle slowing down; bringing water to the boil in an electric kettle.
- Calculate the changes in energy when a system is changed by heating, by work done by forces, or by work done when a current flows.
- Use calculations to show how the overall energy in a system is redistributed when the system changes.

**4.1.1.2 Changes in energy**
- Kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv² (**recall**). Units: J, kg, m/s.
- Elastic potential energy: Ee = ½ke² (**given on the equation sheet**), assuming the limit of proportionality is not exceeded. Units: J, N/m, m.
- Gravitational potential energy: Ep = mgh (**recall**). Units: J, kg, N/kg, m. **In any calculation the value of g is given.**

**4.1.1.3 Energy changes in systems**
- ΔE = mcΔθ (**given**). Units: J, kg, J/kg °C, °C.
- Definition: specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the substance by 1 °C.
- **Required practical 1** (8463 and 8464): determine the specific heat capacity of one or more materials, linking the decrease of one energy store (or work done) to the increase in temperature and the increase in thermal energy stored.

**4.1.1.4 Power**
- Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or the rate at which work is done.
- P = E/t and P = W/t (**both recall**). Units: W, J, s.
- 1 W = 1 J/s.
- Give examples that illustrate the definition, e.g. two motors lifting the same weight through the same height, one faster.

## 4.1.2 Conservation and dissipation of energy

**4.1.2.1 Energy transfers in a system**
- Energy can be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated, but cannot be created or destroyed.
- In a closed system there is no net change to the total energy.
- In all system changes energy is dissipated, stored in less useful ways ("wasted").
- Ways of reducing unwanted transfers: e.g. lubrication and thermal insulation.
- The higher the thermal conductivity of a material, the higher the rate of energy transfer by conduction across it.
- How the rate of cooling of a building depends on the thickness and thermal conductivity of its walls.
- **Students do not need the definition of thermal conductivity.**
- **Required practical 2 (separate Physics only):** the effectiveness of different materials as thermal insulators, and factors affecting insulation.

**4.1.2.2 Efficiency**
- efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer (**recall**).
- efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input (**recall**).
- Calculate or use efficiency as a decimal or a percentage.
- **HT only:** describe ways to increase the efficiency of an intended energy transfer.

## 4.1.3 National and global energy resources
- Main resources: fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas), nuclear fuel, bio-fuel, wind, hydro-electricity, geothermal, tides, the Sun, water waves.
- Renewable: being, or able to be, replenished as it is used.
- Uses: transport, electricity generation, heating.
- Students should be able to:
  - describe the main energy sources available;
  - distinguish renewable from non-renewable;
  - compare ways different resources are used (transport, electricity generation, heating);
  - understand why some resources are more reliable than others;
  - describe the environmental impact of using different resources;
  - explain patterns and trends in the use of energy resources;
  - consider the environmental issues that may arise from their use;
  - show that science can identify environmental issues but not always deal with them, because of political, social, ethical or economic considerations.
- **Descriptions of how resources are used to generate electricity are not required.**

## Boundaries the reviewer enforces

- No definition of thermal conductivity may be demanded.
- No electricity-generation mechanism may be demanded in 4.1.3.
- "Increase efficiency" questions are Higher tier.
- Required practical 2 content is separate Physics only; tag it `separate_only` and keep it out of Combined pools.
- Ee and ΔE = mcΔθ are given: an item may expect selection and use, but not recall. Ek, Ep, P = E/t, P = W/t and both efficiency equations are recall.
- g is always stated in the question (normally 9.8 N/kg; 10 N/kg is also used by AQA in some papers — state it either way).
- Energy transferred electrically (E = Pt, E = QV) belongs to 4.2 Electricity; an Energy item may use E = Pt only if Electricity has been taught (check the concept's taught status) or if the equation is stated.
