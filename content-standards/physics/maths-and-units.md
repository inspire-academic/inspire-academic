## Demand by tier (Inspire convention, consistent with AQA papers)

- **Foundation:** one-step substitution into a recalled or given equation; simple unit conversions (g→kg, kJ→J, minutes→s); efficiency as decimal and percentage.
- **Both tiers:** substitution needing one rearrangement; squaring (½mv²); two-step problems where the first answer feeds the second (e.g. Ep lost → Ek gained).
- **Higher:** rearranging to find v from Ek (square root); multi-step linking of two equations with unit conversions; the student must choose the equation (not named in the stem); standard form both ways.

## Numerical conventions for items

- **Numbers are realistic** for the context: a car of 800–2000 kg at 10–35 m/s; a person of 40–100 kg; a kettle of 1.5–3 kW; g = 9.8 N/kg (or 10 N/kg, stated).
- **Answers:** state the number of significant figures or decimal places expected when it matters; otherwise accept answers within ±1% (numeric items store a tolerance). Intermediate values are not rounded.
- **Units in the answer:** when the unit is part of what is assessed, the student chooses or writes it; otherwise the unit is given beside the answer box.
- **No trick numbers:** avoid values where a common error gives the same answer as the correct method (e.g. m = 2 so ½m = 1 hides a missing ½).
- **Standard form** in KaTeX: `3.2 \times 10^{4}`; students may type `3.2e4`.
- **Templates** must keep every generated instance realistic and must never generate a value where two methods (correct and a named misconception) coincide.
