# Mastery Engine — Content Production Architecture and First Build Phase

Decisions already approved are taken as given: Toolkit → Energy → Electricity; the 14-concept Energy slice (subject to concept-card approval); the concept pack as the permanent production unit; design for thousands and validate with four; build clean prospective evidence and backfill legacy evidence only where it meets the standard.

## 1. Minimum Evidence Inventory vs Scale/Variety Inventory

### 1.1 What the mastery rules actually consume

Per concept, a student needs:

- 1 diagnostic item;
- at least 5 distinct items for "4 of the last 5 correct";
- at least 1 non-MCQ;
- at least 1 item at difficulty band 2 or above;
- at least 1 mastery-check item from a separate pool;
- success in 2 sessions;
- a retrieval item at +7 days and another at +21 days, neither served in the previous 14 days;
- an unlabelled application item.

A student who gets things wrong needs headroom: a fresh item after each failure.

### 1.2 The optimisation: parameterised templates

Most Energy and Toolkit concepts are calculations (Ek, Ep, Ee, ΔE = mcΔθ, P = E/t, efficiency, rearranging, prefixes, standard form).

- **For those concepts, the authored unit is a template, not a fixed question.** One stem gets reviewed; code generates the numbers and context, and code computes and checks every instance's answer. Each misconception distractor is generated by a stated rule, e.g. "forgot to square v" or "used grams not kilograms".
- **What the engine gets:** unlimited fresh practice and retrieval instances at no extra review cost, and a student cannot memorise answers.
- **Adjusted rule for distinctness:** for SECURE, the "last 5" must come from at least 3 distinct *items or templates*. Five instances of one template are not mastery evidence. This keeps the anti-gaming guard while templates supply the volume.

Conceptual concepts (stores and systems, conservation, resources, dissipation) cannot be templated well. They need fixed, misconception-targeted items.

### 1.3 Minimum Evidence Inventory (Phase 1, four students)

| Concept type | Diagnostic | Practice | Mastery check | Retrieval | Units per concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy calculation concepts (kinetic, gravitational, elastic, transfer calcs, SHC, power): **6** | 1 fixed | 2 templates + 1 conceptual MCQ | 2 fixed (≥ 1 numeric/constructed, ≥ 1 band 2) | template instances | **6** |
| Energy mixed concept (dissipation & efficiency): **1** | 1 | 1 template + 2 conceptual | 2 | template instances + 1 fixed | **7** |
| Energy conceptual concepts (stores-systems, conservation, resources): **3** | 1 | 4 fixed | 2 | 1 fixed | **8** |
| Toolkit concepts: **4** | 1 | 2 templates | 2 | template instances | **5** |
| Exam-style application (multi-concept, 3–6 marks, shared across concepts) | — | — | — | — | **5 in total** |

**Total authored units: 36 + 7 + 24 + 20 + 5 = 92**:
- about 16 are the existing approved Energy diagnostic items (re-tagged and re-reviewed);
- that leaves **about 76 new units, of which about 21 are templates**.

In reviewer effort that is well under half the earlier 160-item estimate. And templates give *more* variety than fixed items would.

**Validity is not weakened**: every level rule can be satisfied with fresh, distinct evidence. The only compromise is headroom. A student who fails a concept's mastery check twice exhausts the fixed mastery pool. That is exactly the point at which the engine escalates to the teacher anyway (E1/E2), so the gap never blocks a student.

### 1.4 Scale/Variety Inventory (built continuously, on demand)

The target per concept at commercial scale:
- 3–4 templates;
- 8–12 fixed items across evidence classes;
- 4–6 mastery-check items;
- 6+ retrieval-grade items or templates (two years of spaced checks);
- Foundation-tier variants;
- varied contexts, including African contexts;
- 2–3 application items per concept.

That comes to roughly 25–35 units per concept.

**Expansion is triggered by data, not by schedule.**
- The engine tracks item exposure.
- When any active student's *unseen* pool for a concept and evidence class falls below 2, or any item passes a set exposure count, an authoring task is created for that concept pack.
- Growth therefore follows real use: four students generate almost no expansion work, and 100 students generate exactly the variety they consume.

## 2. The Physics Subject Expert Review Agent

### 2.1 Role and independence

- **An exacting independent reviewer, not a drafter.** It never edits items. It returns a verdict per item with reasons and, for "approve with corrections", the exact correction.
- **Separate context from the drafter.** It never sees the drafter's reasoning or conversation; it receives only the item file, the automated-check report and the reference pack.
- **Blind solve first.** For every item it answers the question itself *before* it is shown the intended key, mark scheme or misconception mapping. A mismatch is an automatic flag. This is the single strongest defence against a reviewer that agrees with a plausible wrong key.
- **Adversarial stance.** Its instructions tell it to look for reasons to reject, to prefer REJECT over rewriting, and that approving a defective mastery-check item is worse than rejecting a good one.
- **Higher-stakes items get a second, independent solver.** For mastery-check and application items, a second independent instance (or a different model, e.g. Codex, if available) also solves each item blind. The two answers must agree with the key.

### 2.2 Implementation

- **Agent definition** in the repo at `.claude/agents/physics-subject-reviewer.md`:
  - most capable model;
  - read-only tools (Read, Grep, Glob), so it cannot modify content;
  - returns a JSON report that the orchestrator stores;
  - versioned — the version is stamped on every report.
- **Reference pack** at `content-standards/physics/`, versioned in git:
  - AQA 8463 statement references and short statement text for the active domains;
  - canonical concept cards and prerequisite edges;
  - the misconception library;
  - Inspire mastery criteria and evidence-class definitions (diagnostic, practice, mastery check, retrieval, exam application, practical);
  - difficulty-band definitions;
  - GCSE mathematical demand (AQA mathematical requirements);
  - the AQA physics equation list, split into recall and given, with symbol and unit conventions;
  - the AQA command-word glossary;
  - mark-allocation principles;
  - accepted terminology (e.g. "energy is transferred", never "used up"; "resultant force");
  - Inspire item-writing standards;
  - the Inspire pedagogical framework.

  Each report records the reference-pack commit it was reviewed against.

### 2.3 Evaluation rubric

Every criterion is scored PASS / FAIL / N/A with a one-line reason on FAIL.

| # | Criterion | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Scientifically accurate | Hard |
| A2 | Blind-solve answer equals the key (numeric: within the stated tolerance and unit) | Hard |
| A3 | Units correct and consistent; conversions stated or required as intended | Hard |
| A4 | Exactly one defensible answer; no second defensible option | Hard |
| A5 | No unintended clue (grammatical agreement, length, "all/never", repeated stem words in the key) | Hard |
| B1 | Assesses the tagged concept (and not mainly a secondary skill) | Hard |
| B2 | Within the specification and the taught sequence; no dependence on untaught material | Hard |
| B3 | Tier and difficulty band correct | Soft |
| B4 | Wording precise, unambiguous, GCSE-appropriate reading level; correct terminology | Soft (Hard if meaning changes) |
| C1 | Each distractor is plausible and maps to an authentic, documented misconception or a named slip; none invented to fill a slot | Hard for mastery-check; Soft otherwise |
| C2 | Feedback for each wrong answer names what the student probably did and the correct method | Soft |
| D1 | Evidence-class fit: a mastery-check item is independent of practice items and not a near-copy of them | Hard |
| D2 | Evidence-class fit: a retrieval item tests recall of the idea in a form distinct from the original learning | Hard |
| D3 | Evidence-class fit: an application item requires transfer (an unlabelled, multi-step or unfamiliar context), not repetition | Hard |
| E1 | Mark allocation matches the command word and demand (AQA principles) | Soft (Hard for application) |
| E2 | Command word used correctly | Soft |
| F1 | Not a duplicate or near-duplicate of an existing item; flagged in the check report and confirmed here | Hard |
| F2 | Copyright: not a paraphrase of any past-paper question; similarity report reviewed | Hard |
| G1 | For templates: the stem is valid across the whole parameter range (no negative heights, absurd speeds, rounding traps); a sample of instances was solved | Hard |

**Decision rules**

- **APPROVE:** no FAIL.
- **APPROVE WITH CORRECTIONS:** only Soft FAILs, each fixable by a stated, surface-level edit that does not change what is assessed or the key.
  - The corrected item goes back through the automated checks.
  - The reviewer then confirms the correction in a short second pass.
- **REJECT:** any Hard FAIL, or any correction that would change the key, concept, evidence class or difficulty. The drafter writes a new item, which is reviewed from scratch.

### 2.4 Calibrating the reviewer (the safeguard your spot checks cannot provide alone)

- **Seeded defects.** Every batch sent to the reviewer contains 2–3 deliberately defective items that it doesn't know are there: a wrong key, a unit error, an ambiguous stem, a clue, an out-of-spec item, or a near-copy of a past-paper question.
  - The orchestrator records whether they are caught.
  - A block is eligible for your spot check only if the reviewer caught all seeded defects in that batch.
  - Seeded items are never published.
- **Dry run first.** Before any new content, the reviewer reviews the 16 existing approved Energy items and a calibration set of known-good and known-bad items. Its findings tell us whether the rubric and reference pack work before we rely on them.
- **Track record.**
  - The Content QA page shows the reviewer's seeded-defect catch rate.
  - It also shows its agreement with your spot checks and with later live item statistics.
  - These numbers decide how much you can trust it, and are what would justify relaxing sampling later.

## 3. Batch review and human spot-check workflow

### 3.1 Pipeline stages

```
DRAFTED ─▶ AUTO_CHECKED ─▶ AGENT_REVIEWED ─▶ IN_BLOCK ─▶ SAMPLED ─▶ APPROVED (served)
   ▲            │ fail           │ reject                       │ fail
   └────────────┴────────────────┴──────── returned ◀───────────┘
```

**1. Draft.** The drafting agent produces a concept pack: items and templates, with concept tags, evidence class, difficulty band, misconception mapping per option, feedback and explanation.

**2. Automated checks (mandatory, code, no AI):**
- schema and required tags present;
- numeric answers recomputed independently;
- template instances generated across the parameter range and every instance verified;
- units and conversions checked;
- KaTeX renders;
- one key, and feedback for every wrong option;
- length-cue check;
- duplicate and near-duplicate check against the whole bank (normalised text plus numeric-structure similarity);
- copyright similarity against the local past-paper corpus (n-gram overlap; near-matches rejected);
- evidence-class quotas for the pack met.

**3. Subject review.** The Physics Subject Expert Review Agent applies the §2.3 rubric to every item, with blind solve and a second solver for high-stakes items. It produces the per-item report.

**4. Block assembly.** A block is one concept pack (or a few small packs). It becomes eligible for sampling when every item in it is either APPROVED or APPROVED WITH CORRECTIONS (corrections confirmed), and every seeded defect in its batch was caught. Rejected items are simply not in the block until redrafted.

**5. Human spot check.**
- The system draws a sample **with a recorded random seed**.
- Sample size:
  - **minimum 3**;
  - **5 for each of the first three blocks** of a new subject or a new version of the reviewer (the reviewer has no track record yet);
  - then 3.
- Stratification:
  - at least one item from each evidence class present in the block (diagnostic/practice, mastery check, application);
  - at least one item the reviewer approved *with corrections*, if any exist;
  - no two items from the same template or near-duplicate cluster;
  - mastery-check and application items weighted to be at least half the sample, because they certify mastery.
- For each sampled item you see:
  - the item as the student will see it;
  - the key, working and mark scheme;
  - the concept and misconception mappings;
  - the evidence class and difficulty band;
  - the automated-check report;
  - the reviewer's report, including its blind-solve answer.

  You mark each **PASS** or **FAIL**, with a one-line reason for a fail.

**6. Decision.**
- **All sampled items pass:** you may **batch-approve** the block. One signed-in action stamps every item in it with you as approver of record.
- **Any material fail:** the block is returned. Nothing in it is approved. Then:
  1. Classify the failure:
     - **isolated:** a defect the rubric would not reasonably catch;
     - **systematic:** a defect class the rubric or reference pack should have caught.
  2. If isolated, fix and re-review that item.
  3. If systematic, update the rubric or reference pack (new reviewer version) and **re-review the whole block**, plus any already-approved blocks from the same reviewer version where that defect could occur.
  4. Draw a **fresh** sample. It excludes items you already passed, and includes the corrected item where relevant.

### 3.2 After go-live: live item quality

The nightly item statistics job extends from the diagnostic to every mastery-engine item. It flags:
- facility too high or too low for its difficulty band;
- negative or low discrimination (strong students missing it);
- a distractor chosen more than the key;
- excessive "not sure";
- repeated teacher overrides on concepts where the item was decisive;
- student or teacher challenges.

A flagged item is **suspended from mastery-check use** (it can still be practice) and goes back to subject review.

With four students these statistics carry no weight. They become meaningful at Phase 2 (15–30 learners), and sooner for diagnostic items taken by free-diagnostic users.

### 3.3 Where you do this

A **Content QA** view added to the existing `teacher/question-review.html` (not a new tool). It has three parts:
- a block list with status;
- the sampled items with reports side by side;
- PASS/FAIL controls and the batch-approve button.

## 4. Architecture changes for review and provenance

### 4.1 One item bank, not five

**New Mastery Engine items live in `diagnostic_questions`, extended, rather than in a new bank.** It is the only bank that already has:
- a misconception per option;
- typed-number answers;
- review states;
- the human-approval trigger;
- version stamps;
- nightly item statistics;
- a server-marked runtime.

A fifth bank would repeat exactly the fragmentation the recommendation set out to end. The name is historical; a view `assessment_items` can present it under a neutral name later.

New columns:
- `evidence_class` (diagnostic / practice / mastery_check / retrieval / application / practical);
- `template_id` (nullable);
- `block_id`;
- `pipeline_stage`;
- `drafted_by` (agent id + model + version);
- `draft_ref` (hash of the drafting brief);
- `marks` (application items).

The existing diagnostic pool filter gains `evidence_class = 'diagnostic'`, so practice and mastery items never leak into a diagnostic.

### 4.2 New tables

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `item_templates` | stem with placeholders, parameter ranges and constraints, answer formula reference, distractor rules; generator code lives in the repo and is referenced by id + version |
| `item_auto_checks` | per item per run: check suite version, results JSON, pass/fail |
| `item_reviews` | per item per review: reviewer agent id + version, reference-pack commit, rubric version, blind-solve answer, second-solver answer, per-criterion results, decision, reasons, corrections |
| `content_blocks` | concept(s), subject, status, reviewer version, seeded-defect results, sample size, random seed |
| `block_samples` | block, item, human result, reason, reviewer (auth user), timestamp |
| `block_decisions` | block, decision (approved / returned), failure classification, decided_by (auth user), timestamp |
| `item_challenges` | challenges from students or teachers ("I think this answer is wrong"), status, resolution |
| `programme_units` | programme, unit, order, concepts, planned dates, actual taught dates (and per cohort) |

**Evidence rows record the instance:** `learning_evidence` gains `template_id` and `instance_params` (seeded), so every templated answer can be regenerated and audited.

### 4.3 Approval stays human-only, enforced in the database

- The existing trigger (approval requires `auth.uid()`) stays.
- A new `approve_content_block(block_id)` function runs **as the signed-in user**. It only succeeds when:
  - the caller is an admin;
  - the block's sample of the required size exists;
  - every sampled item passed, recorded by that same user;
  - every item in the block is AGENT_REVIEWED with an accepted decision.
- It then sets `review_status = 'approved'` and `reviewed_by = auth.uid()` on each item. Agents hold no credentials that can call it.

### 4.4 Display state for taught vs assessed

The student and parent state is derived from `programme_units` plus the engine level. Engine levels are unchanged; this is a display layer.

| Display state | Derivation |
|---|---|
| Not yet taught | concept's unit not yet taught to the student's cohort |
| Taught, not yet checked | taught, no evidence |
| Needs work (INSECURE) | only ever for taught concepts |
| Developing / Secure / Mastered | as the engine computes |

Untaught concepts with diagnostic evidence (the Electricity pre-test) show "not yet taught — we'll use this to plan your lessons". They are never shown as "insecure" or "needs work", and never escalate.
