# Mastery Engine — Pilot Re-scope and Content Operating Model

*28 September 2026 · addendum to `mastery-engine-system-recommendation.md` · status: FOR REVIEW, nothing built.*

Trigger: the ISM Physics sequence is **Week 1 Physicist's Toolkit → Energy → Electricity → … Forces later**, and the cohort is **four students**. Both facts change the pilot; neither changes the architecture much, and the places where they do are instructive.

---

## 1. Recommended first domain

**Energy (AQA 8463 §4.1), with the Week 1 Physicist's Toolkit skills as cross-cutting prerequisite concepts, then Electricity (§4.2).**

Agree with your instinct, for four reasons beyond "it's what's being taught":

1. **Energy is identical in AQA Combined Trilogy and separate Physics.** The only separate-only part of §4.1 is Required practical 2 (thermal insulators), which is tagged and kept out of Combined pools. So the unresolved question of whether any of the four are on Combined Science structures cannot distort the first slice. (It does matter for Electricity: §4.2.5 static electricity is physics-only.)
2. **Energy is calculation-dense** (Ek = ½mv², Ep = mgh, Ee = ½ke², ΔE = mcΔθ, P = E/t, efficiency). Calculations give numeric-answer items, which are the strongest anti-guessing evidence the engine has, and they expose prerequisite gaps (rearranging, units, standard form) cleanly.
3. **It already has the most existing content**: 16 approved diagnostic items across 12 subtopics (4 of them typed-number), two Energy teaching lessons, Energy flashcards, and — crucially — **teacher-marked ISM Week 1 submissions on the toolkit skills** (units and prefixes, standard form, rearranging, graph thinking). That is real prerequisite evidence waiting to be tagged.
4. **Energy shares concepts with Forces** (work done, elastic potential energy, power). Building Energy first forces the concept model to prove it can hold a concept that belongs to two domains — a better test of domain independence than Forces alone would have been.

**Timing, and why it is actually an advantage.** By the time the engine is live (late October at the earliest), the cohort will have been taught much of Energy. That makes Energy the pilot for **verification and retention** (diagnose what stuck, fix it, prove it holds at 7 and 21 days), and makes **Electricity the pilot for the full acquisition loop** — diagnose *before* it is taught, teach, practise, verify, retain. Two domains, two halves of the loop, both on the real timetable. For that, Electricity content must be ready before Electricity teaching starts (question 1 below).

Forces: keep the ~34-concept graph (from the research) as a design artefact; stock it with content later, when the cohort reaches it. No Forces content production now.

| Retrieval spares | 2 | 7/21-day checks without re-serving within 14 days |
| Exam-style application | 1 per concept, realised as ~6 multi-concept items of 3–6 marks | MASTERED |

≈ 13 per concept → **~130 items**.

**Per toolkit concept** (4): diagnostic 1, practice 4, mastery check 3 → **~32 items** (Physics contexts, reusable by Chemistry quantitative work later).

**Misconceptions:** ~12–15 for Energy (e.g. energy "used up"; power confused with energy; efficiency > 100% / efficiency as energy; heat vs temperature; forgetting to square v; mass in grams; h as distance travelled rather than height change), each with correct conception, indicators, linked items and an intervention.

**Interventions:** one per concept (≈ 14), mostly existing — sections of the two Energy lessons and the ISM Week 1 lesson — plus ~5 new short worked examples or misconception clinics.

**Existing and reusable:** 16 approved Energy diagnostic items (tag them to concepts and misconceptions); ISM Week 1 toolkit fields (tag them); Energy lesson sections (reference them; don't copy them). Existing quiz questions on Energy in the live database are unknown until checked.

**Net new to author and review: about 140–150 items, 12–15 misconceptions, 14 concept cards, ~5 micro-interventions.**

**Minimum viable to go live** (loop runs, nothing faked): all 14 concept cards; the 12–15 misconceptions; the Energy diagnostic (~20 items: the existing 16 re-tagged plus ~4–6 new); for each concept at least 4 practice, 3 mastery-check and 1 retrieval item; 6 application items. That is **~110 reviewed items**, of which **~95 new**. Everything above that improves variety, not correctness.

## 4. Content-production operating model

### 4.1 The unit of work is a concept pack

Content is produced and approved **one concept at a time**, as a pack: concept card → misconceptions → items by evidence class → intervention links. A concept goes live only when its pack meets the minimum in §3. This keeps review focused (one idea at a time), makes progress visible ("9 of 14 concepts live"), and stops half-stocked concepts from producing mastery judgements on thin evidence.

### 4.2 Roles

| Role | Who | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Curriculum lead** | Eric | Concept cards, prerequisite edges, "what counts as evidence" per concept, misconception library (final say), final pedagogical approval of each concept pack |
| **Educator reviewer** | 1–2 ISM teachers (or Eric where no second educator is available) | Accuracy and level of every item; distractor plausibility; misconception mapping; blind ratings for validation |
| **Drafting agent** | Claude Code in this repo | Drafts concept cards from the spec text; drafts misconception cards from literature and existing distractor prose; drafts items to each concept's brief; maps distractors to misconceptions; runs all automated checks; prepares review batches |
| **Specialist generator (optional)** | Codex, if the image pilot proves it | Diagrams only, through the same review gate |
| **Approver of record** | a signed-in human only | Enforced by the existing database trigger: nothing is served without `reviewed_by` set by an authenticated user. No script or AI can approve. |

Who drafts what, specifically:

| Artefact | Drafted by | Reviewed by | Approved by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concept cards + prerequisites | Agent (from spec text + your lesson structure) | — | Eric |
| Misconceptions | Agent (literature + existing distractor prose + ISM submission patterns) | Educator | Eric |
| Diagnostic items | Agent | Educator | Educator |
| Practice items | Agent | Educator | Educator |
| Mastery-check items | Agent | Educator | **Eric** (these certify mastery, so they carry a higher bar) |
| Exam-style application items | Agent, to AQA style statistics only | Educator | **Eric** |
| Interventions (links to existing lessons) | Agent proposes | Educator | Eric |
| New micro-lessons / worked examples | Agent drafts or an educator writes | Educator | Eric |

### 4.3 How AI is used safely

- **AI drafts; humans approve.** Approval is enforced in the database, not by convention.
- **Every numeric answer is recomputed by code**, never trusted from the model (the diagnostic builder already does this for typed-number items).
- **Automated checks before a human sees anything:** one key, feedback for every wrong option, every distractor mapped to a misconception or explicitly "slip"; KaTeX renders; no correct-answer-is-longest pattern; units present and consistent; duplicate and near-duplicate check against the whole existing bank.
- **Copyright:** exam-style items are written from the concept and the *statistical* style profile (command words, marks, assessment objectives) only. Past-paper text is never put into a drafting prompt, and every draft is similarity-checked against the local past-paper corpus; near-matches are rejected.
- **No student data in drafting prompts.** Misconception drafting may use *aggregated* patterns ("3 of 4 students chose B"), never names or work.
- **Every AI draft is labelled** (`source = ai_drafted`) and keeps its review history, so the pilot can later compare AI-drafted with human-written item quality.

### 4.4 The human-review gate (reuses `teacher/question-review.html`)

1. The agent submits a batch with the automated check report attached.
2. The educator reviews each item: accurate? right level? do the distractors reflect real student thinking? is the misconception mapping right? → approve / request changes / reject, with a note.
3. Mastery-check and exam-application items go to Eric as the second approver.
4. Eric approves the concept pack as a whole (coverage, balance, evidence requirements).
5. **After go-live:** item statistics (the nightly job) flag items that behave badly. With four students these statistics are meaningless for months, so the free public diagnostic is the source of item data for the diagnostic items; practice and mastery-check items rely on expert review.

### 4.5 Expected throughput

Drafting is not the constraint (the agent can draft a full concept pack in an hour). Review is.

| Activity | Rate (careful review) | Weekly at 5 review hours |
|---|---|---|
| MCQ / numeric items | ~2–3 min each | ~100–120 items |
| Multi-mark application items | ~6–8 min each | (included above, fewer) |
| Misconception cards | ~5 min each | ~10–15 |
| Concept cards | ~10 min each | a full slice in one sitting |

**At about 5 review hours a week, the Energy minimum (~110 items) takes about one week of review; the full inventory (~160) about 1.5–2 weeks.** Electricity is the same again. The number that matters is review hours per week: please commit to one.

## 6. What changes in the architecture

Mostly nothing: the level/evidence/schedule/override split, board-neutral concepts, source-agnostic item tagging, append-only evidence and the `topic_progress` roll-up all carry over unchanged. That is the domain-independence test passing. Four refinements came out of this pass, each of which would have surfaced eventually and is cheaper now:

1. **Concepts can belong to more than one domain.** Work done and elastic energy sit in Energy and Forces; toolkit skills sit under every Physics domain and, later, Chemistry. Replace the single `domain` column with a `domain_concepts` many-to-many (a concept keeps one home domain for display).
2. **Cross-cutting skills are first-class concepts** (`phy.skills.*`) that other concepts depend on. They let the engine separate "doesn't understand kinetic energy" from "can't rearrange an equation", which is one of the most useful diagnoses for a physics teacher.
3. **The programme sequence becomes data.** A small `programme_units` table (programme, unit, order, concepts, planned and actual teaching dates) lets the engine know what has been **taught**. This matters more than it looks: diagnosing an untaught concept as INSECURE is wrong and demoralising. The engine shows "not taught yet", never escalates on it, and orders new learning by the real timetable instead of spec order. It is also what makes "diagnose Electricity before teaching it" a clean pre-test.
4. **Required practicals as an evidence context.** The specific-heat-capacity practical is examinable; ISM practical write-ups tagged to `phy.energy.shc` become a distinct evidence type (`practical`), useful again in Electricity (resistance and I–V characteristics practicals).

**What changes in validation, not architecture: four students.** No statistics about students are possible. The unit of analysis becomes the **student × concept pair** (4 × 14 = 56 pairs for Energy, ~48 more for Electricity):

- engine–teacher agreement on ~100 pairs is estimable, roughly;
- the predictive check (do SECURE/MASTERED concepts pass unseen delayed items?) works per pair;
- the randomised concept order still works within each student.

Treat the pilot as a **feasibility and agreement study**, not an effectiveness study. To say anything about effectiveness, the lab needs more learners by January: free-diagnostic families, other Inspire tutoring students, or a second cohort. Plan for that now rather than overclaim from four.

The teacher queue changes role too: with four students the teacher already knows who needs help. In this pilot its job is to test whether the engine's flags **agree with the teacher's own judgement**. That is the evidence needed before the queue is trusted at 40 students, when the teacher can no longer hold everyone in their head.
