## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

- Choose a suitable acid and insoluble base or carbonate to prepare a named soluble salt.
- Carry out the warm, add-to-excess, filter, evaporate and crystallise method safely.
- Explain why excess solid is added and why the mixture is filtered.
- Distinguish crystallisation from heating a solution to dryness.
- Evaluate common method errors and predict their effects on purity and yield.

Central question: **How can we prepare pure, dry crystals of a soluble salt?**

## PREREQUISITES

- Identify acids, bases, carbonates and neutralisation reactions.
- Name sulfate, chloride and nitrate salts from the acid used.
- Recognise that a soluble product remains in the filtrate.

The lesson will use copper(II) oxide and dilute sulfuric acid as the common worked practical. It will not broaden into titration, solubility curves, water of crystallisation calculations or qualitative ion tests.
